Experiences, not games

The VR arcade is built for score: waves, waves survived, shops bought. An experience asks nothing of you. You arrive, you walk, you look. There is no timer in the museum and no way to lose — the only thing to do is stand in front of a frame and get closer to it than a flat page allows.

That makes these pages the calm end of WebXR: good for a five-minute break, good for showing someone what a browser can do without an app store, and good on a phone in a pinch.

🥽

In a headset

Open the page in the headset's browser (Quest Browser, Pico, or a PC headset through Chrome or Edge with a runtime) and press Enter VR. Walk with the thumbstick and aim the controller laser at a frame or a screen.

🖥️

On a desktop

WASD or the arrow keys walk, click and drag to look, and a click on a frame opens it full size. There is nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.

📱

On a phone

The museum swaps in a thumbstick for walking and a drag-to-look area for everything else. Tap a frame to inspect it; landscape gives you the widest view of a wall.

Controls at a glance

The same defaults for every experience, so you only learn them once.

WhereMoveLook and inspect
Headset Thumbstick on either controller; the local-floor reference space means your real height is your height. Turn your head and body, point the controller ray at a frame or portal screen, squeeze the trigger.
Desktop W A S D or the arrow keys. The wing buttons in the top bar teleport you instead of walking. Click and drag to look, or Lock Mouse for pointer-lock. Click a frame to inspect it; Esc closes it.
Phone Left thumbstick, while a second finger drags elsewhere to look — the two never fight. Tap a frame to inspect it, and tap ✕ or press Esc-equivalent to close. Rotate to landscape first.

Questions people ask

Do I need a VR headset?

No. Every experience here has a full desktop and phone mode built from the same scene. A headset adds depth, hand tracking and a sense of scale; it does not unlock anything.

Why does the museum ask for permission?

WebXR needs your consent before it can draw to a headset, and the browser may ask before going full screen. Nothing is recorded, uploaded or sent anywhere — the whole scene is generated in your browser.

Can I visit on a phone?

Yes. The museum detects a touch device, shows a virtual joystick and lets you drag the rest of the screen to look. It is the easiest way to see what the space is like before borrowing a headset.

Who makes these?

Artify Space is the artist's own museum — the artworks, the portfolio links and the branding are theirs. Digital Infohub builds the browser version and hosts it. The three portfolio screens link out to their official site, Fab store and ArtStation.

Is any of this paid or account-based?

No. There is no signup, no purchase and no account. Nothing is stored on a server, and the only thing kept between visits is whatever your browser chooses to cache.

What is coming next

Two ways to play, one link

You do not need a headset to try any of this. Every VR game here ships with a desktop mode built from the same scene, so you can look around, aim and shoot — or drag, orbit and build — with the keyboard and mouse you already have. If a headset is connected, a VR button appears and the game switches to your controllers.

🥽

In a headset

Open the page in the headset's browser (Quest Browser, Pico, or a PC headset through Chrome or Edge with a runtime installed) and press Enter VR. Controllers give you aiming, triggers and haptics.

🖥️

On a desktop

No headset, no problem. Choose the desktop simulator and play with mouse aim, WASD or drag, and the keyboard. The same waves, shops and scores are all there.

📱

On a phone

Touch controls work for the tycoon game and the menus, and mobile browsers can often enter cardboard-style VR. For the shooter, a desktop or headset is the better experience.

Questions people ask

Do I need a VR headset?

No. Both games have a full desktop mode. The headset adds hand tracking, depth and comfort; it does not unlock content.

Why do some games ask for a browser permission?

WebXR needs permission before it can draw to a headset, and spatial audio needs a tap before sound starts. Nothing is recorded, uploaded or sent anywhere — the games run entirely in your browser.

Can I play on a work laptop?

Yes, if it has a reasonably modern graphics chip. If frames drop, close other tabs or lower the window size; the renderer caps pixel ratio so it stays playable.

Is any of this paid or account-based?

No. There is no signup, no purchase and no account. High scores and tycoon saves live in your browser's local storage on your device.

What is coming next

The VR arcade is deliberately small for now: two games that we can keep polished rather than a wall of half-finished demos. Next up are more WebXR cabinets in the same style — a seated puzzle game, a rhythm title and a room-scale exploration piece.
